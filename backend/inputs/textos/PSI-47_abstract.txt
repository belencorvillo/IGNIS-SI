HYPOTHESIS:
The TSI P-Trak operates by condensing isopropanol around aerosol particulates that are subsequently laser counted. It was hypothesized that the P-Trak would not work in microgravity due to two potential failure modes that exist. 1) the system would clog or become obscured by isopropanol, and 2) the internal air-flow dynamics of the optics chamber of the device will cause an over counting of particulates. The TSI P-Trak was modified for microgravity use via using capillary action to recycle the isopropanol. Therefore, there are two basic hypotheses: 1) that the P-Trak, as modified for use in SAME, would function normally in microgravity. 2) that the aerosol created and filtered per the experiment design would be within the measurement capabilities of the test devices.

RESEARCH IMPACTS / EARTH BENEFITS:
Extended duration space flight missions with larger vessels will require fire detection systems with increased reliability and sensitivity. This research will impact the development of novel fire and smoke detection systems for the International Space Station and future spacecrafts. Additionally, the successful adaptation of the TSI P-Trak for microgravity can inform the design of instruments for use in other challenging Earth environments, such as submarines or underwater laboratories where accurate detection of smoke can save lives.
